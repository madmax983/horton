    How it works

    1. The simulated device sends frames and keeps every one the logger has not acknowledged. After a crash it sends them again.
    2. The Worker records each frame as one horton WriteBatch: one WAL block, written and flushed to OPFS before the acknowledgement goes back.
    3. Every tick, one bounded compaction step. When tables hold only ticks older than 3,000, they are archived: staged by horton, stored in IndexedDB (a strict transaction), and only then committed away from flash. The wait for IndexedDB falls between two horton calls.
    4. On start, horton recovers the flash as the device would after a power cut: the newest manifest copy, then the WAL up to any torn tail.
